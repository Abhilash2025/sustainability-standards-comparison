01 · Architectural Overview

Two frameworks, two logics.

SBTi is a voluntary private standard targeting science-based decarbonisation. ESRS E1 is a mandatory EU disclosure standard under CSRD, supplemented by the CSRD Voluntary Annex for undertakings below 1,000 employees.

02 · Governance & Leadership Accountability

Net-zero starts in the boardroom.

SBTi places the strongest governance obligations on companies. ESRS E1 embeds governance within ESRS 2 GOV. The CSRD Voluntary Annex has no governance-specific disclosure beyond basic information.

Governance · CNZS-C1
Board sign-off, not just sustainability sign-off
Your highest governing body must formally own SBTi targets.
C1.1
"The company's highest governing body (i.e., Board of Directors or equivalent) shall agree to assume overall accountability for SBTi targets, including setting and submitting targets for validation, and overseeing target implementation."
C1.2
"Companies shall submit and report a description of the governance structures responsible for overseeing and implementing their targets."
C1.3
"Companies shall establish documented processes for reporting progress, periodic review and adjustment of targets to reflect evolving science, company performance, and best practice."

ESRS E1 equivalent: ESRS 2 GOV-1 requires disclosure of governance structures but does not isolate climate-target accountability.

CSRD Voluntary: B1 only asks for governance body name — no equivalent depth.

Transition Plan · CNZS-C2
A transition plan, due at validation
Every company needs a Board-approved plan describing how it will deliver targets.
C2.1
"Transition plans shall describe the actions and corresponding timeframes required to implement the company's targets in a manner consistent with reaching net-zero by no later than 2050."
C2.2
"Transition plans shall be formally approved and adopted by the company's highest governing body."
C2.4
"Category A companies shall publish a disclosure of their transition plan within 15 months of completing Target Validation."

ESRS E1-1 para 12: Transition plan must include GHG targets, decarbonisation levers, key actions, investments and funding, board approval, and EU Climate Law alignment.

ESRS E1-1 para 12(b): CapEx amounts for coal, oil and gas required — SBTi does not mandate this level of fossil-fuel disclosure.

Recommendation · R1.2
Policy engagement consistency
Lobbying must align with climate targets.
R1.2
"Companies are recommended to ensure that public policy engagement, lobbying activities, and advocacy efforts are consistent with and supportive of their climate targets. Companies are recommended to follow best-practice frameworks such as InfluenceMap..."

ESRS gap: ESRS 2 GDR-P covers climate-related policies but has no lobbying-specific requirement.

Recommendation · R2.3
Transition plan alignment with ESRS
SBTi explicitly references ESRS E1-1 in its recommendations.
R2.3
"Companies are recommended to develop transition plans in line with international standards such as the Transition Planning Taskforce (TPT) Disclosure Framework. When applicable, companies must ensure compliance with applicable national or subnational regulatory frameworks, such as the disclosure requirements set out in ESRS E1-1..."

This is a direct interoperability bridge: SBTi recommends alignment with ESRS E1-1 voluntarily.

03 · Target Setting: Scope 1, 2, 3 & Net-Zero

Where the frameworks diverge most sharply.

SBTi is prescriptive on ambition levels, methods, and coverage. ESRS E1 is disclosure-focused: it requires companies to report targets but does not mandate specific ambition levels.

Scope 1 · C10–C11
Absolute or intensity to residual
Near-term targets must cover 100% of Scope 1 emissions.
C10.1
"Near-term targets shall cover 100% of scope 1 emissions."
C10.2
"Companies shall set one or more near-term targets using: a) Scope 1 absolute emissions reduction; b) Scope 1 emissions intensity reduction; c) Asset transition targets."
C11.2
"Long-term targets shall cover 100% of scope 1 emissions."

ESRS E1-6 para 24: "The undertaking shall disclose the GHG emission reduction targets it has set, including absolute GHG emission reduction targets for scopes 1, 2 and 3..."

ESRS gap: No method prescription — only disclosure requirement.

Scope 2 · C12–C13 + C24
Hourly matching introduced
Scope 2 near-term target required with hourly matching option.
C12.1
"Near-term targets shall cover 100% of scope 2 emissions."
C12.2
"Companies shall set one or more near-term targets using: a) Low-carbon electricity alignment targets; b) Scope 2 absolute emissions reduction."
C24.1
"Companies shall report a description of the activity pool-level or sector-level actions taken..."

ESRS E1-8 para 34: Location-based Scope 2 mandatory; market-based optional. No hourly matching requirement.

SBTi advantage: Hourly matching (C24) is more granular than any ESRS requirement.

Scope 3 · C14–C16
≥67% coverage for Category A
Category A companies must cover at least 67% of Scope 3 emissions.
C14.1
"Near-term targets shall cover at least all scope 3 categories that individually represent 5% or more of the company's scope 3 emissions in categories 1–14."
C16.1
"Long-term targets shall cover 100% of scope 3 emissions."
C16.2
"Scope 3 long-term targets shall be set to reach residual levels in line with applicable net-zero pathways."

ESRS E1-8 para 35: Scope 3 required if material, with phased-in reliefs.

Gap: SBTi ≥67% coverage threshold likely exceeds ESRS materiality threshold.

Net-Zero · C17
Validated long-term target by 2050
Companies must set long-term targets to reach residual emissions and neutralise any remainder.
C17.1
"Companies (including Category B companies) shall set near-term targets on scope 1, scope 2, and scope 3 emissions."
C17.2
"Companies shall set long-term targets to reach residual scope 1, scope 2, and scope 3 emissions."
C17.4
"To reach a state of net-zero, the company shall: a) Reduce scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions to zero or to a residual level consistent with eligible net-zero pathways; and b) Neutralize any residual emissions at the net-zero target date."

ESRS E1-1 para 11: Transition plan must be compatible with limiting global warming to 1.5°C and achieving climate neutrality by 2050 per EU Climate Law.

ESRS gap: No explicit validated net-zero target mandate — only transition-plan compatibility.

Base Year · C4 + C8
Most recent comprehensive year
Base year selection with 5% significance threshold for recalculation.
C4.1
"Companies shall select the most recent year for which comprehensive emissions and other applicable data are available as the target base year for near-term targets."
C8.3
"Companies shall apply a 5% significance threshold to determine whether recalculation is required."

ESRS 1 Chapter 7: Comparative information required; restatements for acquisitions/divestments with phased reliefs.

Gap: SBTi recalculation triggers are stricter (>5% threshold) than ESRS reliefs.

Target Revision · C20
Trigger-based revision
If targets are non-conformant after recalculation, they must be revised and resubmitted.
C20.1
"If, following a target base year recalculation or other significant changes, targets are determined to be non-conformant, companies shall revise those targets and submit them for validation."

ESRS 1 para 7.6: Restatement rules for major acquisitions/divestments — but no target-validation-driven revision requirement.

04 · Implementation Hierarchy, Actions & Carbon Credits

Direct abatement first, then market instruments.

SBTi V2.0 introduces a strict implementation hierarchy. ESRS E1-9 creates a disclosure requirement for carbon credits but explicitly bars their use toward GHG reduction targets.

Hierarchy · C21
Activity → pool → sector
Document that direct abatement has been assessed and implemented before relying on pool or sector-level actions.
C21.1
"Companies shall document and demonstrate that activity-level actions have been assessed and implemented, where feasible, before relying on activity pool-level or sector-level actions."
C21.2
"Where emissions arise within an activity pool, companies may take actions within the same activity pool from which the emissions-generating activity arises."
C21.3
"Where sufficient action cannot be taken at the activity or activity pool level due to structural constraints, companies shall document constraints and may take action at the sector level."

ESRS gap: No equivalent hierarchy in ESRS E1-5. SBTi C21 is unique among the frameworks.

Carbon Credits · C25–C28
Prohibited toward targets
Carbon credits cannot count toward SBTi targets. ESRS E1-9 requires granular credit disclosure.
C25.1
"Activity matching: Companies shall demonstrate that actions taken correspond to the same activity type, product, material, fuel, or energy source as the company's underlying activity."
C26.2
"Excluded mechanisms: The following shall not be considered eligible for target implementation: a) Emissions reductions derived from hypothetical scenarios; b) Emissions reductions or removals, including GHG credits, used for the SBTi's Ongoing Emissions Responsibility program; c) GHG credits sold or otherwise transferred to a third party; d) Emissions reductions estimated ex ante and not yet realized."

ESRS E1-6 AR 12: "The GHG emission reduction targets shall be gross targets, meaning that the undertaking shall not include GHG removals, carbon credits or avoided emissions as means of achieving the GHG emission reduction targets."

ESRS E1-9 paras 39–44: Requires granular disclosure of carbon credits purchased, cancelled, share from removal projects, and integrity standards used.

Bioenergy · C26
Sustainability criteria for bio-based feedstocks
No net carbon accounting assumptions. Chain-of-custody documentation required.
C26.1
"Companies shall account for and report forest, land, and agriculture (FLAG) emissions; bioenergy emissions; and both biogenic and technological removals in line with the GHG Protocol Land Sector and Removals Standard, ensuring separation of emissions and removals."

ESRS E1-8 para 31: "The undertaking shall disclose its direct biogenic CO2 emissions from combustion or biodegradation of biomass separately from scope 1 GHG emissions."

Gap: ESRS only requires separate reporting; SBTi requires chain-of-custody documentation and sustainability criteria.

Electricity · C29
Electricity-specific hierarchy
Reduce demand first, procure renewable second, apply hourly matching third.
C29.1
"Reduce demand → procure renewable → hourly matching."

SBTi C29 establishes an electricity-specific implementation hierarchy: reduce demand first, procure renewable energy second, apply hourly matching third. ESRS E1-8 has no electricity-specific hierarchy.

Scoring Methodology

Methodology
Depth Comparison Scoring Framework
Click to expand the full scoring methodology, scale, sources, validation and limitations.

1. Purpose: To enable a grounded, repeatable assessment of how prescriptively each framework addresses specific climate-related themes. This update strengthens the “Depth Word Chart + Methodology” by separating disclosure coverage from prescriptive / conformance depth. This avoids treating ESRS disclosure requirements as equivalent to SBTi validation or implementation criteria.

Scores are qualitative and do not indicate legal equivalence, regulatory compliance sufficiency, or assurance equivalence. ESRS/CSRD compliance requires ESRS-specific materiality, boundary, presentation, assurance and disclosure assessment.

2. Scale: Use two scores where helpful: (A) Disclosure Coverage Score and (B) Prescriptive / Conformance Score. Keep the same 1–5 scale: 1 = minimal / absent; 2 = high-level or conditional; 3 = moderate; 4 = detailed; 5 = highly prescriptive or granular.

3. Themes selected: 24 themes covering governance, target-setting (Scope 1/2/3, net-zero), implementation (hierarchy, carbon credits, bioenergy), assurance, cross-cutting ESRS concepts (double materiality, scenario analysis, financial effects), and six new themes: deforestation-free supply chains, investment actions (CapEx/OpEx), bioenergy/biofuel sourcing, product attributes/traceability, claims framework, and market-based instruments in Scope 3.

4. Scoring process: Each theme was scored by reading the operative provisions of all three source documents line-by-line. For SBTi: criteria (C) and recommendations (R) were mapped. For ESRS E1: disclosure requirements (E1-1 to E1-11) and general requirements (ESRS 1, ESRS 2 GDRs) were mapped. For CSRD Voluntary Annex: Basic Module (B) and Comprehensive Module (C) disclosures were mapped.

5. Source documents: (a) SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2.0 (June 2026); (b) Revised ESRS June 2026 — Commission Delegated Regulation C(2026) 5010, Annex I; (c) Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/5011 Annex — Voluntary Standard for undertakings below 1,000 employees.

6. Validation: Scores were cross-checked against extracted structured data (JSON criteria maps) to ensure textual grounding. No scores were inferred from secondary sources.

7. Limitations: (a) Scores are qualitative judgments, not quantitative metrics. (b) ESRS depth is sometimes distributed across multiple DRs (e.g., governance in ESRS 2, targets in E1-6), which may understate apparent depth if viewed only through E1. (c) CSRD Voluntary Annex is proportionate by design; low scores reflect intent, not deficiency. Scores do not indicate legal equivalence, regulatory compliance sufficiency, or assurance equivalence. ESRS scores should primarily be read as disclosure-depth scores, not as prescriptive/conformance equivalence to SBTi validation.

Two-dimensional scoring note: ESRS scores should primarily be read as disclosure-depth scores unless the standard explicitly imposes a method, prohibition, or disclosure condition. ESRS is detailed on transition plans, targets, GHG emissions, carbon credits/removals, and anticipated financial effects, but does not provide SBTi-style target validation. Low CSRD Voluntary scores should be described as proportionate / conditional by design, not as a deficiency. Scope 3 and transition-plan disclosures are conditional in the voluntary standard.

Disclaimer Scores are qualitative and do not indicate legal equivalence, regulatory compliance sufficiency, or assurance equivalence. ESRS/CSRD compliance requires ESRS-specific materiality, boundary, presentation, assurance and disclosure assessment in accordance with the official publications.

Baseline assumption: Company is ALREADY SBTi-compliant. Timelines measure ADDITIONAL work for ESRS E1 + CSRD Voluntary.

Deadline: First ESRS reporting: FY2027 for large EU undertakings (reports published 2028). Timelines counted backward.

Effort definition: Person-time, not calendar time: High = 1–6 person-months (cross-functional, complex modelling, external consultants); Medium = 2–6 person-weeks (data collection, minor system changes); Low = 1–5 person-days (formatting, one-line disclosures).

Effort factors: Data availability, external dependencies, cross-functional coordination, analytical complexity, assurance readiness.

Validation: Scores cross-checked against extracted structured data. No scores inferred from secondary sources.

Disclaimer The Low / Medium / High effort bands are not prescribed by ESRS, CSRD, SBTi, EFRAG or audit firms. These estimates should be refined after one reporting and assurance cycle using actual effort, audit queries, review cycles and remediation findings.

Companies using only CSRD Voluntary will need significant uplift to meet SBTi validation standards.

Key Takeaways

Implication
SBTi → ESRS E1 is largely a disclosure exercise
Target data, GHG inventory and transition plan content transfer with minimal gap.

SBTi-aligned companies already collect the data required by ESRS E1-6 (targets), E1-8 (GHG emissions) and E1-1 (transition plan). The main additional work is formatting disclosures to ESRS structure, adding financial effects (E1-11) and scenario analysis (E1-2).

Implication
ESRS → SBTi requires ambition uplift
Disclosure alone does not guarantee validation.

ESRS E1 requires disclosure of targets but does not validate their ambition or methodology. A company could disclose targets that are not science-based and still comply with ESRS. SBTi validation adds the ambition benchmark (1.5°C or well-below 2°C pathway) and method-specific rules (absolute contraction, sectoral decarbonisation approach).

Implication
CSRD Voluntary is a starting point, not an endpoint
Undertakings below 1,000 employees receive proportionate relief, not exemption.

The CSRD Voluntary Annex is designed to help SMEs provide comparable climate data to larger counterparts. It does not replace SBTi or full ESRS alignment for companies seeking credible net-zero claims. Scope 3 is encouraged but not mandatory, and financial effects are not required.
